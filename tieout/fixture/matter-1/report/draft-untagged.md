# Expert Report of Dana Example

## Brindlecote Valve Works, Inc. v. Ostmarren Logistics Corp.

Prepared at the request of counsel for the plaintiff and dated November 6, 2026.

## 1. Assignment

Counsel for Brindlecote Valve Works, Inc. ("Brindlecote") asked me to calculate the profits Brindlecote lost when Ostmarren Logistics Corp. ("Ostmarren") ended the parties' supply agreement before its term expired. I was not asked to decide whether the termination breached the agreement, and I assume for this report that it did.

I reviewed 41 documents produced by the parties, the deposition testimony of two witnesses, and the shipment and cost data Brindlecote's finance department prepared. My opinions are my own, and my firm is paid for my time at its standard rates regardless of the outcome.

I am a certified public accountant and have worked on commercial damages for more than twenty years, mostly in supply and distribution disputes. I have testified in deposition and at trial on lost profits, mitigation and the discounting of future losses. My qualifications and the list of materials I considered are in the appendices to this report.

## 2. Summary of opinions

1. Brindlecote's lost profit on the minimum purchase commitment for the rest of the term, before mitigation, is $4.8 million (model.csv!B9).

2. After deducting the profit Brindlecote expects to earn on replacement orders, its net lost profit is $3.6 million (model.csv!B11).

3. At a discount rate of 9.5% (model.csv!B12), the present value of that net lost profit is $3.42 million (model.csv!B14).

## 3. Background

Brindlecote manufactures Series K precision valve assemblies, and Ostmarren distributes industrial flow components to fleet and terminal operators. The parties signed a Master Supply Agreement on March 14, 2022 (PX-1 at 1) for a term ending on March 13, 2025 (Id. at 5).

The agreement set the price at $118.40 per unit and required Ostmarren to buy a minimum of about 4,800 units (PX-1 at 3) in each calendar month. It describes that commitment in plain terms: "The Minimum Purchase Commitment is the essence of this Agreement . . . in reliance on it" (Id.). The agreement also allows no termination for convenience and records that "the Supplier's lost profits on the Minimum Purchase Commitment ... are a foreseeable consequence of a wrongful termination by Buyer" (Id. at 6).

A First Amendment dated December 9, 2022 amended the agreement of 14 March 2022 and raised the price to $121.75 per unit from January 1, 2023 (PX-2 at 1). Nothing else in the agreement changed.

Ostmarren was an important customer. At the board meeting of February 14, 2023 (PX-5 at 1), Brindlecote's chief financial officer, Tamsin Prewett, reported that Ostmarren accounted for about 12% (Id. at 2) of net revenue for the year. The board kept a dedicated Series K line, with a second shift, in place for the full term.

## 4. The termination

In June 2023, Ostmarren's vice president of procurement, Ruben Esterby, wrote to Brindlecote that "[Ostmarren's] terminals will keep drawing Series K at the contract rate" (PX-6). Ms. Prewett testified that Brindlecote kept the second shift on the line because of that message, at a cost of roughly $1.1 million (Prewett Dep. 13:9) a year in labour.

Ostmarren's own records tell a different story. Its internal forecast, issued on 2023-04-03 (DX-102 at 1), planned only four thousand eight hundred units from Brindlecote for Q4 2023 (Id. at 2) and none for the first quarter of 2024. It assumed a second supplier would finish qualification by mid-September 2023 (Id. at 3). Mr. Esterby confirmed that the forecast was issued on April 3, 2023 (Esterby Dep. 33:7-8) and was not shared with Brindlecote.

Ostmarren's procurement committee met on July 11, 2023[^1]. The committee deck put Ostmarren's spend with Brindlecote at $7.01 million (DX-104 at 3) a year and estimated Ostmarren's exposure for lost profits at $3 million to $5 million (Id. at 5). It recorded Brindlecote's on-time delivery for the first half of the year at 97.6% (DX-104 at OSTL0004017), and it recommended that Ostmarren "[i]ssue notice by the end of September and cite delivery performance as the reason for exit" (DX-104 at 7). Asked about that line, Mr. Esterby testified that "I would not say late deliveries were the reason" (Esterby Dep. at 32:3-4).

Ostmarren signed a supply agreement with a replacement supplier, Garrowfield Flow Controls LLC, on August 17, 2023, with first deliveries scheduled for November 2023 (DX-103 at 1). Garrowfield quoted $109.90 (Id. at 2) per unit, a saving of 9.7% (Id.) against the Brindlecote price, or about $683K (Id. at 3) a year at the minimum commitment. Garrowfield's parts passed qualification on 2023-09-15[^2].

Ostmarren's supplier scorecards rated Brindlecote's on-time delivery at 97.9% (OSTL 0004020 to OSTL 0004021) for the second quarter. Ms. Prewett testified that on-time delivery was above 96% (Prewett Dep. 14:14) in every quarter of the account. In Mr. Esterby's words, "Garrowfield offered a lower price per unit, and the committee wanted to move the volume" (Esterby Dep. 31:20-21).

Ostmarren sent its notice of termination on 2023-09-28, effective Oct. 31, 2023 (DX-101 at 1). The notice gave as its reason that “[c]ontinuing the relationship is not in its commercial interest” (Id.). Brindlecote received it on September 28, 2023 (Prewett Tr. 14:7), and Ms. Prewett testified that “The notice talked about late deliveries, and that was simply not what our own records showed” (Id. at 14:10-12). Mr. Esterby explained the timing this way: "The notice was timed so that we would have supply … We could not leave the terminals without parts" (Esterby Dep. 32:20-22).

## 5. Shipments before the termination

Brindlecote's net revenue from Ostmarren in the first, partial year of the account was $5,306,925 (PX-3 at 2), at the original price of $118.40 (PX-1 at 3) per unit. Ms. Prewett testified that the shipment data cover every Series K shipment to Ostmarren through October 31, 2023 (Prewett Dep. 15:9-11), and that returns were too few to matter.

Monthly shipments to Ostmarren ranged from 4,610 to 5,240 units (PX-3 at 3) over the life of the account. Through the end of October, Brindlecote shipped 48,237 units (Id. at 2) to Ostmarren in the final year, month by month 4,890 (shipments.csv!B11), 4,815 (shipments.csv!B12), 4,966 (shipments.csv!B13), 4,872 (shipments.csv!B14), 4,920 (shipments.csv!B15), 4,847 (shipments.csv!B16), 4,780 (shipments.csv!B17), 4,610 (shipments.csv!B18), 4,812 (shipments.csv!B19) and 4,725 (shipments.csv!B20) units from January, for net revenue of $5.87 million (PX-3 at 2). The final shipment left the plant on Oct. 31, 2023 (Id.).

Shipments first fell below the minimum in July, at 4,780 units (Prewett Dep. 12:16-17). Ms. Prewett raised the shortfall with Mr. Esterby on August 4, 2023 (Id. at 12:20), and he called it a "timing issue" at one terminal. The low point came in August at 4,610 units (shipments.csv!B18). September shipments were 4,812 units (shipments.csv!B19), billed at $585,861 (shipments.csv!C19) on an invoice dated September 29, 2023 (PX-4 at 1). Over the three months before the notice, shipments averaged 4,734 units a month.

The August invoice, dated 8/31/2023, covered 4,610 units for $561,267.50 (PX-4 at 1). The last regular invoice is dated 10/31/2023 (Id. at 2). October fell short of the minimum by seventy-five units, and the shortfall invoice for $9,131.25 (Id.) remains disputed.

## 6. Ostmarren's stated reason for the termination

The notice blamed delays in delivery, and the record does not support that reason. Ostmarren's own scorecard for Q2 2023 rated Brindlecote a preferred supplier with a defect rate of 0.31% (OSTL0004020). For the third quarter it rated on-time delivery at 96.8% and the defect rate at 0.27% (OSTL0004021). Mr. Esterby testified that delivery performance was "mentioned in the deck" (Esterby Dep. 32:1), and that price was the reason for the change.

The committee approved the exit on July 11, 2023 (DX-104 at 8) and asked Ostmarren's chief operating officer to sign the notice once Garrowfield's parts were qualified. The next day, on 2023-07-12, Mr. Esterby wrote that "[t]he committee's decision is made and we move to Garrowfield" (OSTL0004022). He did not tell Brindlecote. Ostmarren's forecasts, by its own account, had been within 4.2% (DX-102 at 4) of actual purchases, so the cut it planned for the fourth quarter was a decision rather than a guess.

The agreement allows termination for cause only if a breach is not cured "within sixty days after receiving notice describing the breach in reasonable detail" (PX-1 at 5). Ms. Prewett testified that Brindlecote "never received a notice of breach or a request to cure" (Prewett Dep. 14:18-19). I therefore measure damages over the rest of the term, as counsel instructed.

## 7. Lost profits

I measure lost profits as the revenue Brindlecote would have earned on the minimum purchase commitment for the rest of the term, less the costs it avoided by not producing those units, less the profit it earned on replacement orders. Section 4 explains why I treat the termination as ending all purchases.

The damages period runs from the first month after the effective date through the last full month of the term, 16 months (model.csv!B2) in all. At the minimum commitment, Brindlecote lost sales of 76,800 units (model.csv!B4) over that period. At the amended price, lost revenue is $9.35 million (model.csv!B6).

The parties' joint cost analysis puts the variable cost of one unit at $58.92 (Ex. 12 at 3), which matches Brindlecote's own variable cost study at $58.92 (BVW0000126). The study excludes fixed overhead and depreciation, which do not change with volume over the remaining term[^3]. Avoided variable cost over the damages period is $4,525,056 (model.csv!B8), which leaves a lost profit before mitigation of $4,825,344 (model.csv!B9). That is a margin of 51.6% on the lost revenue.

The Series K line can produce 7,400 units (PX-7 at 2) a month on two shifts. After the termination, Brindlecote offered the released capacity to other customers, and its operations team expects replacement orders through the end of the period to earn incremental profit of $1,203,100 (Id. at 3). I deduct that amount in full as a mitigation offset of ($1.2 million). Ms. Prewett testified that the replacement volume "is spread across several smaller accounts" (Prewett Dep. 15:24-25), and no single customer took over the Ostmarren orders. The capacity could not be fully resold because Brindlecote's other customers order a different seat material[^4].

Ostmarren's terminals kept drawing roughly 4,900 units (Esterby Dep. 33) a month after the switch, a figure its own demand memo states as roughly 4,900 units (OSTL0004023). Ostmarren held 6,150 units in its terminals on the effective date and expected network demand of 58,800 units (OSTL0004026; OSTL0004024) for the following year. Nothing in that record suggests that Ostmarren's need for Series K units fell.

I discount the net lost profit at Brindlecote's weighted average cost of capital of 9.5% (Prewett Dep. 13:24). The board tests projects over a range of 8.5% to 10.5% (Id. at 13:24-14:1), and footnote 5 explains why I use the midpoint[^5]. At 9.5% (model.csv!B12), the present value of the net lost profit of $3,622,244 (model.csv!B11) is $3,418,710 (model.csv!B14).

## 8. Conclusion

On the record I reviewed, Ostmarren's purchases would have continued at the minimum commitment for the rest of the term. Brindlecote's lost profit before mitigation is $4,825,344 (model.csv!B9), its net lost profit is $3,622,244 (model.csv!B11), and the present value of that loss is $3,418,710 (model.csv!B14). Ostmarren's committee expected a saving of $682,560 (DX-103 at 3) a year from the switch and weighed it against the exposure its own deck described.

Ostmarren issued a litigation hold on October 2, 2023 (OSTL0004025), and its account manager's log records the request for samples for a second supplier on 2023-06-12 (BVW0000128-29). I have relied on the documents as produced.
